01 / The Story

An idea that became an aircraft.

This project started as a digital design and developed into a physical flying machine. The process involved CAD, printing, cutting, building, wiring, programming and testing.

01

The original design

I started by designing the aircraft and its components using AutoCAD. This allowed me to plan the shapes and dimensions before cutting any material.

02

Turning drawings into parts

After designing the parts, I printed the templates and used them to cut the physical components out of foamboard and balsa wood.

03

Adding the electronics

The aircraft was equipped with a brushless motor, electronic speed controller, LiPo battery, FS-i6 transmitter and receiver, and multiple servos.

04

Testing and improving

The project didn't stop at the first version. I tested the aircraft, learned from the results and continued improving the design and build.

03 / Construction

Built from lightweight materials.

The aircraft structure was designed around lightweight, accessible materials that could be cut and assembled by hand.

▱

Foamboard

Used for larger lightweight structural sections. Foamboard made it possible to create large surfaces without adding excessive weight.

⌁

Balsa Wood

Used where additional stiffness and structural support were needed while keeping the aircraft lightweight.

CAD

AutoCAD

The parts were digitally designed first so the shapes could be printed and transferred accurately onto the physical materials.

04 / Electronics

The electronics behind the aircraft.

The RC system converts the pilot's inputs into motor power and mechanical movement of the aircraft's control surfaces.

⚡

Brushless Motor

Provides the thrust required to propel the aircraft. Brushless motors are well suited to RC aircraft because they provide high power while keeping weight relatively low.

◈

ESC

The Electronic Speed Controller receives the throttle command and controls the power delivered from the battery to the brushless motor.

🎮

FS-i6

The FlySky FS-i6 transmitter is the pilot's interface. It sends commands to the receiver using the RC radio link.

📡

Receiver

Receives the commands from the transmitter and sends control signals to the ESC and servos.

⚙

Servos

Servos convert electrical control signals into physical movement, allowing the aircraft's wings and control surfaces to respond to pilot input.

🔋

LiPo Battery

Supplies the electrical energy needed by the propulsion system and other electronics.
